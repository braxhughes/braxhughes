## Braxton Hughes

CS student at **WSU Vancouver**. Equipment technician at a **semiconductor fab**. Before both
of those, I sold things for a living — Apple hardware, then cars.

I build small, local-first macOS apps, and I open-source the unglamorous tooling I write to
build them.

---

### What I'm building

**Peat** — a Mac utility that keeps a local index of what you worked on, in files you can read
and delete. On-device capture, OCR and summarization are in testing on macOS 27. Not shipped,
no waitlist, nothing to sign up for yet. *(Private repo until launch.)*

**Moss** — a calm, local-first daily dashboard: calendar, money, meals, news, email and notes
on one quiet screen. No account, no cloud, no ads.
→ **[mossprint](https://github.com/braxtonhughes360-ship-it/mossprint)**

I only claim what I've actually observed. Peat runs against a written claims register — every
public sentence about it has to be backed by a test result, and the ones that aren't proven
yet don't get said.

### The combination

A fab floor runs on SOPs, root-cause analysis, and the assumption that undocumented work
didn't happen. Sales runs on hearing what someone actually needs instead of what you'd like
to sell them. Both turn out to be the parts of software engineering that nobody teaches you
in a CS course — and both are why my repos are full of verify steps, evidence folders, and
claims that have to earn their way into a README.
